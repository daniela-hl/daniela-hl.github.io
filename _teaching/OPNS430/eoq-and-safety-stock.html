Cycle service level counts cycles without a stock-out. Fill rate counts bags served on time — it is always the friendlier number, which is why the two are so easy to confuse.

What it costs — the financial tree, per store per year

Adding lead time costs nothing — we just order earlier, and the average inventory is unchanged. Adding variability costs real money, because the safety stock sits in the warehouse all year long.

How to read this
  • The people strip above the chart is one mark per customer walking into the store. A week with more people makes the inventory line fall faster — the slope is the demand rate.
  • Each little step down in the inventory line is one customer buying. Zoom in (small window) to see the steps; zoom out and they blur into the straight diagonal from the slides.
  • The amber band is a lead time: the stretch between placing an order and the truck arriving. The only thing that decides a stock-out is how far inventory falls inside that band.
  • Every band produces one dot on the right panel. That is where the bell curve comes from — it is not an assumption, it is the pile of realised lead-time demands.
  • The ROP line is the cut. Everything to its right in the right panel is a stock-out. Drag ROP and watch the histogram stay exactly where it is while only the cut moves.
  • Turn the randomness dial to 0 to get the perfect textbook sawtooth, then turn it up slowly.
